How It Works: The EOQ Formula

The Economic Order Quantity formula calculates the batch size where ordering costs and storage costs intersect at minimum expense.

EOQ = sqrt((2 × Annual Demand × Order Cost per order) / Holding Cost per unit per year)
  • Annual Demand (units/year): Total annual customer demand.
  • Order Cost (per order): Fixed purchasing and handling fee.
  • Holding Cost (per unit/year): Annual storage, capital, and insurance cost.

Worked Example (Step-by-Step)

An enterprise sells 12,000 units/year. Placing an order costs 150 in local currency, and storing 1 unit costs 4.50/year.

Step 1: Multiply 2 by Demand and Order Cost 2 × 12,000 × 150 = 3,600,000
Step 2: Divide by annual Holding Cost (4.50) 3,600,000 / 4.50 = 800,000
Step 3: Calculate square root √800,000 = 894.43 units

Solved Result: Order 894 units per replenishment order.

How It Works: The Reorder Point Formula

Calculates total consumption expected during supplier lead time, plus safety stock.

Reorder Point = (Average Daily Usage × Lead Time in Days) + Safety Stock
  • Average Daily Usage (units/day): Normal sales or consumption rate.
  • Lead Time in Days: Calendar turnaround days for new delivery.
  • Safety Stock (units): Dedicated buffer against stockout risks.

Worked Example (Step-by-Step)

A retail warehouse sells an average of 45 units/day with an 8-day supplier lead time and maintains 120 buffer units.

Step 1: Calculate Lead Time Demand (45 × 8) 45 units/day × 8 days = 360 units
Step 2: Add Safety Stock (360 + 120) 360 units + 120 units = 480 units

Solved Result: Trigger replenishment order when inventory touches 480 units.

How It Works: The Safety Stock Formula

Measures the spread between worst-case lead-time demand and normal expected lead-time demand.

Safety Stock = (Max Daily Usage × Max Lead Time) − (Average Daily Usage × Average Lead Time)
  • Max Usage × Max Lead: Worst-case scenario inventory requirements.
  • Avg Usage × Avg Lead: Normal expected inventory consumption.

Worked Example (Step-by-Step)

Daily usage averages 45 units (peaks at 65 units). Lead time averages 8 days (stretches to 12 days).

Step 1: Max Usage × Max Lead (65 × 12) 65 × 12 = 780 units
Step 2: Avg Usage × Avg Lead (45 × 8) 45 × 8 = 360 units
Step 3: Subtract Avg Demand from Max Demand 780 − 360 = 420 units

Solved Result: Hold 420 units of safety stock in reserve.

How It Works: The Inventory Turnover Formula

Divides annual Cost of Goods Sold by the average inventory value maintained in the warehouse.

Inventory Turnover = Cost of Goods Sold / Average Inventory Value
  • Cost of Goods Sold (per year): Direct cost of goods sold.
  • Average Inventory Value: Mean inventory holding valuation.
  • Days Sales of Inventory (DSI): 365 ÷ Turnover ratio.

Worked Example (Step-by-Step)

A company records annual COGS of 1,800,000 with an average stock value of 225,000.

Step 1: Confirm Average Stock Value Beginning + Ending Stock / 2 = 225,000
Step 2: Divide COGS by Average Stock (1,800,000 / 225,000) 1,800,000 / 225,000 = 8.00 turns/year
Step 3: Calculate Days Sales of Inventory (365 / 8) 365 / 8.00 = 45.6 days

Solved Result: Inventory turns over 8.00 times per year (every 45.6 days).
